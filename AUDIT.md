# Comrade — Repository Audit & Improvement Plan

_Audit date: 2026-07-10._
_Method: full manual read of every Rust crate, the CLI, the Tauri shell + desktop JS, and the Android app, cross-checked by an 8-dimension parallel agent sweep. Architecture findings were additionally adversarially verified (all CONFIRMED); every other finding below was verified by direct file reads unless explicitly flagged otherwise._

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**Overall health: C+ (prototype grade: promising core, credibility gaps at the edges).**
The Rust workspace shows genuinely strong engineering discipline for a prototype — clean crate layering, typed errors everywhere, ~90 behavior-asserting unit tests, an encrypted-at-rest store with adversarial "scan the disk for plaintext" tests, and CI that gates fmt/clippy/test. The problem is the distance between what the README promises and what is actually wired: three of the seven advertised engines (Saathi mesh, NIP-65 gossip routing, encrypted media) are never invoked by any frontend; the Android app cannot unlock the vault at all, so its voice "post"/"timeline" commands can never succeed; and DMs use the deprecated, unauthenticated NIP-04 scheme in a product whose entire identity is "privacy-first."

**Top 3 risks:** (1) NIP-04 (AES-CBC, no authentication, metadata-leaking) for E2E DMs while NIP-44 is available in the already-pinned nostr-sdk; (2) Off-Grid mode is a *false privacy assurance* — the UI tells the user "public relays paused, Saathi mesh active" while relay websockets stay connected and no engine is ever disconnected; (3) `change_pin` re-encrypts the store non-atomically — a crash mid-way permanently corrupts user data.

**Top 3 opportunities:** (1) close the honesty gap — wire engine lifecycle to the state machine (or mark features experimental) and make the README match reality; (2) close the CI and release blind spots — Android tests and the Tauri crate are never built in CI, and official release APKs ship debug-signed with ephemeral keys and without the voice model; (3) scope the Sabha feed subscription (currently the global Kind-1 firehose) to a follow set, which fixes performance, UX, and privacy in one change.

## Decision log

- **2026-08-04 (owner, four in-chat actions, and two of them needed a boundary drawn):** *"in chat actions (play something together e.g /spotify Kun Faya kun), invoke tara (@tara what does she @xyz thinking of herself), assign tasks e.g (/task get some work done @xyz) and assign in app actions (/assign @breath). feel free to re label any you think for"* — the composer could only send text, while everything the app can *do* sat behind a screen you had to leave the conversation to find. Four commands landed, plus the grammar behind them (`comrade_core::command`, 43 tests) and one new engine (`comrade_core::karya`, 18). `docs/CHAT_ACTIONS.md` is the design record. Six things are worth recording here rather than leaving in the diff. **(1) The grammar went into Rust, not into three frontends.** The app already had one command parser — `VoiceCommand.kt` — and `/pay`'s regex already existed *four* times (`vault.rs`, the desktop JS, the Dart fake, the CLI) and had drifted. So the composer grammar is one module with one answer, and both shipping frontends call it. What did **not** happen is the tempting corollary: `VoiceCommand.kt` still has its own grammar, because folding it in is a refactor of a tested parser this container cannot run tests for, and doing it blind would have been trading a real risk for a tidiness win. Named as follow-up, not done. **(2) The hard part of a slash grammar is what is *not* a command.** A message swallowed as an unknown command is worse than one that arrives confusing, because the sender believes they sent it — so `20/80 split`, `see /Users/me/notes`, a pasted `/usr/local/bin` and `/Notes` all stay text, by four separate rules with four tests, and a command-shaped-but-unknown token is *reported* rather than sent. **(3) `/assign` was relabelled, and the verb was dropped entirely.** You cannot assign somebody a breath: the breathing screen's own copy says *"you don't have to feel better yet"* and its `strings.xml` carries a comment forbidding claims the app cannot support, so an imperative was the wrong register. `/breathe` opens it here; `/comrade-breathe @ana` offers it to her — the action *is* the command, `comrade-` means "for my comrade", and there is one fewer concept than any verb would have added. The owner specified the received copy and it is used verbatim: *"Your comrade asked you to take a deep breath — they are here for you"*. Two rules now hold every offer line and a test enforces both: it says **what the sender did, never what the reader is** (the app has no idea whether anybody is anxious), and the tail is **the sender's own claim**, which is the one reassuring thing this app is actually in a position to pass on. Gated comrades-only and behind the **shared** `nudge::nudged_recently` cooldown — the same floor-on-notifications argument the entry above records for the breathing screen's trigger — and `offer_action` **returns the count** so a swallowed send says *"they were told recently"* instead of looking broken. It deliberately does not reuse the `comrade_nudge` envelope, whose guarantee is that it carries no reason at all. **(4) The owner's own Tara example is the case the guard exists for, and this is the one place the request was not implemented as literally worded.** *"@tara what does she @xyz thinking of herself"* asks the companion to characterise a named third party. `tara::mentions_third_party` now sits in front of the engine where `detect_distress` sits, and an aside naming a person gets the askable question back rather than an answer — *"I can't tell you what someone else thinks or feels — I'd only be making it up, and it wouldn't be fair to them. What I can stay with is your side of it: what's coming up for you about @xyz?"*. Two reasons, both from `docs/TARA.md`'s non-negotiable gates: inferring what a real person thinks of themselves is a psychological assessment of somebody who has not consented, is not in the room and cannot correct it (gate 1); and `ReflectiveCompanion` is a **cue-word template matcher**, so asked about a third party it would emit a fluent, confident sentence containing no information, about a human being the reader knows. The owner was asked and chose "guard, plus a reframe hint", which is what shipped. The rule is deliberately blunt — *any* aside naming a person turns around, with no cue list deciding which questions count as assessments, because a fuzzy matcher guarding a hard boundary fails on exactly the cases that matter. Bluntness costs almost nothing: the redirect is also the right reflective move, so the safety property and the product behaviour are the same sentence. `detect_distress` still runs first, and a test pins that ordering. The aside itself is seeded with **only what the user typed** — never the chat history or the peer's messages, which would make the other person an unconsenting participant. **(5) Audio acquisition: the owner asked for the Antra chain including acquisition, and one mechanism was declined.** `comrade_core::catalogue` builds the identity half (a `CatalogueResolver`, defaulting to MusicBrainz — chosen because it needs no API key, so there is no credential to ship in a binary or have revoked out from under every install) and a real four-tier acquisition ladder (`choose_audio_plan`): **this device → the person you are talking to (`share`) → an archive whose licence permits it → embed-only**. What is absent, and absent by design rather than unbuilt, is Antra's last two tiers: DRM-protected service streams (defeating a technological protection measure is a liability *distinct* from infringement — DMCA §1201, EU InfoSoc Art. 6, India's Copyright Act §65A) and its mirrors/Soulseek (unlicensed redistribution, and a Comrade running a mirror would be the backend `share` exists to avoid). `docs/TOGETHER.md` §9 had already ruled both out; this records that the code matches, with the exit condition stated — a licensing arrangement or a source serving unencrypted audio under terms permitting redistribution, **not** a better downloader. The concrete outcome for the owner's own example is worth stating: `/spotify Kun Faya Kun` resolves identity through the catalogue, then finds it in your library, receives it from the peer, or plays the YouTube embed. It works; it just does not take the DRM path. The licence check runs **before** the fetch (a tier that downloaded first and inspected afterwards would already have made the copy it was deciding about) and lives in `choose_audio_plan` rather than in the adapter, so a sloppy or hostile resolver returning an all-rights-reserved URL changes nothing — pinned by `a_catalogue_that_offers_licensed_audio_cannot_smuggle_it_past_the_gate`. **(6) Zero new `BridgeEvent` variants, and that constraint shaped the whole design.** A variant would have meant an exhaustive Kotlin `when`, *two* exhaustive Dart switches, and regenerating `frb_generated.rs` — which `flutter_rust_bridge_codegen` was not available to do here, so half of it would have been hand-edited, against this repo's own rule that the file is generated and never edited. So a task and an offer ride marker-keyed JSON envelopes like every other control payload, **one message each**, and the receiver renders its own chat bubble from the envelope through the existing `IncomingDirectMessage` (reusing the real event id, so plain-chat dedup catches a redelivery). The bubble is stable English that `parse_task_line`/`parse_offer_line` read back, and each frontend localises from the parsed action — if the wire format were the human sentence, translating it would stop two phones in different languages reading each other's threads. Tasks additionally learned a product rule: **a task is a request, not a write to somebody else's list.** An assignment arrives `Open` and can be **declined** as a first-class outcome; an assigner may withdraw but never complete; nothing reopens (a returning task is a new task, because "done, no wait, not done" is an argument two devices would have to arbitrate and neither has standing to win); and an assignment is accepted only from an already-accepted conversation, because a stranger who can push rows onto your list has been handed a harassment channel. Task ids are minted by the assigner rather than derived from the DM's event id — the first idea, and wrong, because the assigner does not know that id until the relay accepts the send and the two sides see different ids anyway. **Verification**, on a container where `rustup update stable` was run first (1.94.1 → **1.97.1**, which is exactly the trap `CLAUDE.md` records — a clean `-D warnings` - **2026-07-31 (media sharing was down, and the reason was a single dead host):** A field report — "still not able to share any media" — with a screenshot of the shipped Compose app reading `Send media failed: v1=http error: error sending request for url (https://cdn.hackers.town/upload)`. Three separate defects, one of them total. **(1) The upload host was one hard-coded string** (`DEFAULT_BLOSSOM_SERVER = "https://cdn.hackers.town"`) and it had stopped completing TCP connections. `curl -m 12` to it returns nothing at all (exit 28, no HTTP status), which is exactly the transport error the screenshot shows — so *every* attachment, on *every* frontend, had been failing for as long as that host had been down, with no way for a user to route around it. It is now a list — `DEFAULT_BLOSSOM_SERVERS` — tried in order, and the ordering is measured rather than guessed: `examples/blossom_probe.rs` performs a real signed BUD-01 upload to each host and fetches the blob back, and reported `nostr.download` **accepting anonymous uploads with a byte-identical round trip**, `blossom.band` rejecting us (415 for opaque bytes, 400 for a declared type) and `cdn.satellite.earth` answering 401. The two that reject us stay in the list as fallbacks — they cost nothing while the first host works, and being down to one host is precisely the condition that caused this. **(2) There were no timeouts anywhere in the media stack.** `reqwest`'s default is to wait forever, so the failure mode above could equally have been a spinner that never resolved; upload and download now use a 10-second connect budget (a dead host must cost seconds, not minutes, before the next is tried) and a 120-second transfer budget (a 10 MB attachment on a phone uplink is slow, but not unbounded). **(3) The upload contradicted its own privacy comment.** The line above it read "upload ciphertext only — the host sees opaque bytes", and then it sent the *plaintext's* MIME type as the request's `Content-Type` — telling the media host whether the user had just sent a photo, a voice note or a PDF, about a body that is AES-GCM ciphertext and could not be any of them. Uploads now declare `application/octet-stream`; the real type never leaves the E2E envelope (the NIP-94 event that carries it is built for its id and never published). Separately, the `v1=` in that error message is uniffi's rendering of a variant's *field names*, which had been reaching users verbatim for every error in the Compose app — `ComradeCore.rethrowing` now goes through a `UiException.humanMessage()` that mirrors the Flutter app's `describeUiError`, so both frontends word the same failure the same way. **Verification:** `cargo fmt`/`clippy --workspace --all-targets`/`cargo test --workspace` green, including three new hermetic tests (the scalar default cannot drift from the list; a misconfigured uploader falls back to the defaults instead of becoming unable to send; a failover names *every* host it tried, exercised against refused loopback ports so it needs no network). The end-to-end proof is the probe example, which is deliberately **not** a test: it needs the internet, and a CI job that fails when someone else's server is down is a job people learn to ignore. **Not fixed here, and named as the follow-up:** the host list is still not user-configurable. Today's measurement is one day's measurement, and the durable answer is a settings field like the TURN one — the difference between "broken until the next release" and "change the host". It needs a new bridge call on both ABIs and UI in three frontends, which is a feature rather than an outage fix.

- **2026-07-29 (media sharing + screenshots):** Two owner asks, one pass. **(A) Media sharing had a real data-loss bug**, of the same shape as the `send_dm` one the outbox fixed: `upload_and_send_media` encrypted the file, uploaded the blob, persisted the local `MediaRef`, and *then* published the reference DM — and if a relay refused that DM it returned `Err` with the ref already stored. The sender saw a failure **and** saw the attachment in their own thread; the recipient never learned the blob existed; nothing was left to retry from, and the upload was paid for. The reference is ordinary DM content, so it now queues in the same `dak::outbox` as text, keyed by its NIP-94 event id, and the flush loop delivers it when a relay comes back. Two details are load-bearing: the flush loop must **not** re-key a media envelope into a stored message (`rekey_stored_message` would create a text row whose body is the envelope JSON — a chat bubble full of machine-readable noise, and the chat list's preview), and the reaping pass captures each queued item's peer *before* pruning, because a media reference has no stored row to look its conversation up from. Second bug, also visible: `conversations()` and `message_requests()` derived a thread's newest item from stored *messages* only, so a conversation whose only content was an attachment **did not appear in the chat list at all**, and a thread whose newest item was a photo showed a stale text line. Both now fold the media tree in with a `📎 …` preview. Third, hardening: the caller's MIME type is validated and lower-cased (a peer sending `IMAGE/JPEG` used to miss every frontend's `startsWith("image/")` and render as an unopenable file), captions are stripped of control characters and bounded, and an empty payload is refused *before* the upload rather than after it. **(B) Screenshots are now allowed across the app by default**, reversing M1-6's window-wide `FLAG_SECURE`. The justification in the code was "screens can display key material"; checked against both frontends, **no screen renders a secret key** — what is shown is an npub, which is public by construction and printed next to "names repeat, keys don't". So the flag blocked ordinary uses (screenshot a plan, keep a journal entry, attach a picture to a bug report) to protect something that was not there, while stopping no realistic threat: anyone holding the phone can photograph the screen. Blocking is now a user setting, off by default, stored once in `mullu.comrade.ScreenSecurity` and read by *both* frontends so they cannot disagree — deliberately outside the encrypted store, because the flag must be right for the first frame, before any unlock. The scoped mechanism (`setSecureWhileVisible`, reference counted; Dart's `SecureScreen`) ships in exactly one place: the vault passphrase field **while the user has revealed it**, which is the one genuinely secret thing on screen anywhere in the app. **(C) The Flutter app's media seams are no longer stubs.** New `mullu.comrade/media` channel: `ACTION_OPEN_DOCUMENT` picker, camera photo/video, audio playback via a `MediaDataSource` over the decrypted bytes, and "open in another app" via a new `InMemoryMediaProvider` that serves a *seekable* descriptor from memory (`StorageManager.openProxyFileDescriptor`, API 26+ — a pipe would fail the PDF readers and players this exists for). The Compose app had to write decrypted media to `cacheDir/media` for `MediaPlayer`/`VideoView`/`ACTION_VIEW` and pay for it with a purge that a missed call site turns into plaintext on disk (S-4); the unified app now has **no disk plaintext path at all** — the one unavoidable file, a camera capture, is read once and deleted in a `finally`. Received video, which reported "not wired up" forever, is handed to the system player from memory; a real in-app video player is still unbuilt and is named as such. Voice notes come back (D13 reversed in substance, not in gesture: tap-to-record, not press-and-hold, because the gesture was what had no meaning with a mouse). **(D) The composer is Telegram's**, per the owner: emoji inside the field on the left, paper clip inside it on the right, and one round button outside that is Send whenever there is text and otherwise the swappable mic/camera. Every control is capability-gated from a single startup probe, so a device with no camera app never shows a camera button. The emoji picker is hand-rolled (~220 lines, no new dependency — every pub alternative ships a font or drags in `shared_preferences`) and inserts at the caret, not at the end. **(E) Desktop leak**: the SPA minted an object URL per viewed attachment and never revoked one, so every decrypted attachment stayed in the webview heap for the process's life; now a bounded, revocable LRU, cleared on unlock. **Verification honesty:** `cargo fmt --check` and `clippy --all-targets` clean; `cargo test --workspace --locked` green (393 passed, 1 ignored), of which `comrade_ui`'s lib is **97** (the new cases cover the queue-on-refusal path, the envelope that must never become a chat bubble, an expired reference reported against its conversation, the chat-list/request previews, MIME normalisation and caption sanitising). On the Flutter side a stable SDK was fetched for this pass, so for the first time these claims are *run*, not reasoned: `dart format` byte-identical to CI's, `flutter analyze --fatal-infos` **no issues**, `flutter test` **169 passed / 4 skipped** (was 104). `node --test desktop/ui/*.test.mjs` is **60** (was 54) — the six new ones pin the object-URL cache's eviction and revocation, which is the leak fix. **The Kotlin half is compiled** — an SDK, NDK 28.2.13676358, cargo-ndk v4 and both Android Rust targets were installed for this pass, and `flutter build apk --debug` completes: a 189 MB debug APK carrying `libcomrade_jni.so` for arm64-v8a (13.5 MB) and x86_64 (15.2 MB). It took two fixes to get there, both in the new provider and both worth recording because a review had missed them: `uri.lastPathSegment` (a `String?`) was being handed to `ConcurrentHashMap.remove`, whose key is non-null; and a `MatrixCursor` row built with `map { … }.toTypedArray()` inferred its element type to the intersection `Comparable<*> & Serializable`, which Kotlin 2.x will not reify. After those, one `e:` and zero `w:` across the whole module. **What is still unverified is everything at runtime**: no device, no emulator, so not one attachment has been picked, played or opened out, and `FLAG_SECURE` has never been applied to a real window. `desktop/src-tauri` remains uncompilable here (no GTK/webkit2gtk), so the SPA change has `node --check` and its own unit tests behind it and no rendering behind it at all.

Root cause: the one-shot Kind-0 publish raced the relay dials and was never retried (fixed — bounded connect-wait, retry with backoff, republish on every launch), and search fanned the NIP-50 filter across non-search relays (fixed — dedicated search relays, client-side match filter, direct npub lookup). - **2026-07-16:** Voice-model availability reworked (closes M1-7/S10, fixes a latent bundled-model bug). (a) The fetch script's sha256 pin is now committed — computed from a mirror download whose sha512 also matches Alpine aports' pin of the same `alphacephei.com` zip (two independent sources, same bytes). (b) Root-caused the field report "Voice unavailable: model-en-us/uuid": vosk-android's `StorageService` requires an `assets/model-en-us/uuid` marker the fetch script never wrote — so even model-baked APKs failed, and CI APKs (which never run the script) shipped no model at all. The script now writes the marker (and heals an existing staging). (c) APKs without a bundled model now offer a Google-style on-device download prompt on the first voice tap (Settings tap-to-talk / "Hey Comrade" enable, journal dictation): one-time ~40 MB fetch with progress/cancel/retry, sha256-verified against the same pin, staged then atomically moved under `filesDir/voice-model`, picked up by every recogniser via the shared `VoskModel` holder (the wake-word service now uses it too instead of a private unpack). The shared model is reference-counted and closes ~30 s after the last recogniser releases it, so turning voice off reclaims its RAM; it stays resident only while the wake word is enabled or a dictation is in flight.
- **2026-07-12 (owner, supersedes the 2026-07-10 entry above):** D1's sled migration is back in scope and has landed — `comrade_storage` now persists to `redb` instead of `sled`, keeping the identical Argon2id + AES-256-GCM envelope. `EncryptedStore::open` transparently migrates a pre-existing sled store in place on first open (decrypt under the caller's PIN, re-ingest into a fresh redb file, archive the old sled files under `sled-archive/` inside the same directory) — no caller (CLI, JNI, Tauri) changes. `sled` stays a dependency *only* for that one-time migration reader (`comrade_storage::migrate`); it is no longer used for the live store, so `deny.toml`'s sled-transitive-dep ignores now carry an updated exit condition (remove once the migration reader itself is retired). - **2026-07-12 (owner):** M1-1 lands as full NIP-17/NIP-59 gift-wrap, not plain NIP-44-in-Kind-4 — resolving OQ4 in favor of metadata privacy over interop with older plain-NIP-44 clients. `VaultEngine::send_dm_reply` builds every new DM via `EventBuilder::private_msg` (Kind-14 rumor → Kind-13 seal → Kind-1059 gift wrap, signed by a one-time key); the inbox subscription now takes both `Kind::GiftWrap` and legacy `Kind::EncryptedDirectMessage`, decrypting each with the appropriate path, with the `since()` window widened by NIP-59's 2-day timestamp randomization so a fresh gift wrap can never be dropped as "too old." - **2026-07-15:** Six connectivity/calling tickets (COMMS-01–06, referenced by that tag throughout the touched code) close gaps this document already named — §8.1's "always-on background delivery needs a connection service" note, OQ10's TURN question, §3.3's unbounded-feed-subscription finding, and the calls signaling layer's untested call-setup races. **COMMS-01** (background connectivity): `GlobalScope` is gone from `ComradeCore`'s class initializer; `initializeEventBridge()` is now a `suspend fun` awaited by `ComradeApplication.appScope` at startup and, idempotently, by `unlockVaultTyped`, closing a race where an event published before a listener subscribed was silently dropped (a zero-receiver `tokio::sync::broadcast` send is a no-op). A new opt-in `RelayConnectionService` (foreground, `dataSync` type, default-on toggle in Settings) becomes the sole consumer of the event queue once the vault unlocks, and stops on lock/logout; `ChatEventRouter`, not `MainActivity`, now owns notification routing, so an Activity recreation can never double-register a listener or double-notify. - **2026-08-03 (owner: "research whether two counts or four, and proceed"):** Asked at the end of the entry above, and the literature answers it: **two.** A hold after an exhale is not the mirror of a hold after an inhale, though a box treats them as equals. Tests pin that every offered length lands on a boundary, that the last second of a run is `HOLD_EMPTY` (the property the `MIN_SCALE` snap depends on), that no chip drifts more than half a cycle from its own label, and that a zero or negative minute count still gets one whole breath rather than opening on the completed state. `breathingProgress`'s KDoc and its test both lost the "the clock outlives the sit" justification — the clamp survives on the other reason, that shortening the duration moves the total under `elapsed` in one tap. **Verification:** `./gradlew test` **347 JVM tests, 0 failures** (`BreathingScreenTest` 16 → 20 cases, plus the 2-case `BreathingLinePairingTest`), run locally on the tree merged with `main` at b1b4330 — the Android SDK is absent from this container, so this is the Kotlin actually compiling rather than a claim left to CI. Rust, desktop and Flutter untouched. **Not verified:** nobody has sat through a stop. Whether ending at 56s reads as "that was my minute" or as being cut short is a body's answer — **The second symptom in the same message — "the same message is still there for both breath in and breath out" — is a stale install, and the release timeline proves it rather than argues it.** Auto-release cuts a patch per merge, so: **v0.0.71** (03:10Z) carried #80, **v0.0.72** (06:37Z) carried #81, and **v0.0.73** (07:09Z) is the *first* release containing #82's paired lines. ## 2. Repo Map

**Purpose.** "Comrade" — a privacy-first, cross-platform social client over Nostr, with an off-grid libp2p mesh mode and an encrypted couple's shared ledger. Hindi-derived nomenclature: public post = *Chitthi*, engines = *Sabha* (public feed), *Vault* (E2E DMs), *Saathi* (mesh), *Sakha/Sakhi* (couple CRDT ledger).

**Stack.** Rust 2021 workspace (tokio, nostr-sdk 0.44, libp2p 0.56, yrs 0.21, redb 4, aes-gcm/argon2/secp256k1) · Kotlin + Jetpack Compose Android app with an offline Vosk voice assistant · Tauri 2 desktop shell with a no-build vanilla-JS SPA · interactive CLI harness. ~11k lines total. Built through a series of AI-assisted PRs (milestone-labeled commits), no releases yet.

**Architecture sketch.**

```
                    ┌────────────── frontends ──────────────┐
   CLI (src/main.rs)   Android (Kotlin ⇄ comrade_jni)   Desktop (JS ⇄ Tauri commands)
                    └───────────────┬────────────────────────┘

                    ┌───────────────┴────────────────────────┐

```

**Key directories.**

## 3. Audit Report

Severity: **C**ritical / **H**igh / **M**edium / **L**ow. Each finding is labeled **[fact]** (verifiable in code) or **[judgment]** (design opinion).

### 3.1 Security & Cryptography

| # | Sev | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | ~~H~~ | **[fact, resolved 2026-07-12]** DMs used deprecated NIP-04. `send_dm_reply` now builds NIP-17 gift-wrapped (NIP-59) NIP-44 messages via `EventBuilder::private_msg` — content, real sender, and exact timestamp are all hidden from relays; only the recipient `p` tag and a randomized (±2 day) timestamp are public. Legacy NIP-04 decrypt is kept, read-only, so a peer's older DMs still open. See M1-1 and the decision log. |
| S2 | **H** | **[fact] `change_pin` is not crash-safe.** `crates/comrade_storage/src/lib.rs:207-238` re-encrypts every value in place under the new key (lines 214-227) and only afterwards rewrites the salt + verification token (230-233). A crash mid-loop leaves a store whose meta says "old key" but whose values are a mix of old- and new-key ciphertexts — the mixed values are permanently undecryptable under either PIN. No test simulates this. (Latent today — `change_pin` has no non-test callers — but it is the published re-key API.) |
| S3 | **M** | **[fact] Weak-PIN reality vs. "encrypted at rest" promise.** The unlock secret is called a "PIN" throughout (`EncryptedStore::open(path, pin)`, CLI `unlock <PIN>`); nothing enforces minimum entropy (desktop `main.js:207` only checks non-empty). Argon2 uses library defaults (`lib.rs:255`, argon2 0.5 ⇒ m=19 MiB, t=2, p=1 — OWASP minimum). A 4-6 digit PIN over a 10⁴–10⁶ keyspace is offline-brute-forceable in seconds regardless of Argon2. The at-rest encryption is only as strong as the passphrase policy that doesn't exist. |
| S4 | **M** | **[fact] Social graph stored in plaintext keys.** `comrade_storage` encrypts values only; tree keys are plaintext by design (`lib.rs:23-25`). Contacts are keyed by npub (`repository.rs:143`), so a device-level attacker reads the full contact list without the PIN. The header note says "callers that need key confidentiality should hash keys" — no caller does. |
| S5 | **M** | **[fact] Secret-key exposure surfaces.** Android `KeygenSection` renders the full `nsec` on screen with no masking and no `FLAG_SECURE` (`MainActivity.kt:284-290`); the CLI prints the first 20 chars of the nsec (`src/main.rs:592-594`) and 16 bytes of a DH shared secret (`src/main.rs:617-621`); the nsec crosses the JNI boundary inside a hand-rolled JSON Java `String` (`comrade_jni/src/lib.rs:113-117`), which is immutable and unzeroizable. `KeyProfile.nsec` / `StoredIdentity.nsec` are plain `String`s — the README's zeroization claim (`README.md:171`) is true only for the storage key. |
| S6 | **M** | **[fact] Tauri CSP disabled.** `desktop/src-tauri/tauri.conf.json:20` sets `"csp": null` in a webview that renders content from public relays. Mitigation: `desktop/ui/main.js` is disciplined — all dynamic text goes through `textContent` (`el()` helper, `main.js:35-51`), and `innerHTML` is only used for clearing. CSP is the missing second layer, cheap to add. |
| S7 | **M** | **[fact] `allowBackup="true"`** (`AndroidManifest.xml:17`). ADB/cloud backup can exfiltrate the app's data dir. Values are encrypted, but combined with S3 (weak PIN) a backup is an offline brute-force target; privacy-focused apps conventionally set `allowBackup=false`. |
| S8 | **M** | **[judgment] Sakha has no forward secrecy.** Pairing derives one static symmetric key from static-static ECDH (`sakha.rs:136-144`, `crypto.rs:78-104`) and publishes ciphertext to public relays as Kind-30078. Compromise of either partner's nsec at any future time decrypts the couple's entire ledger history retained by relays. Acceptable for a prototype only if documented as a known limitation; there is no threat-model doc (see N2). |
| S9 | L | **[fact] Sakha sync handler trusts the relay's author filter.** `sakha.rs:217-260` never checks `event.pubkey == partner_pk`; authenticity rests entirely on AES-GCM decryption succeeding. AEAD makes forgery infeasible, but replayed/reflected updates are accepted (mostly harmless — Yrs updates are idempotent). Cheap defense-in-depth check missing. |
| S10 | L/M | **[fact] Vosk model fetched without integrity pinning.** `scripts/fetch-vosk-model.sh:12,29` downloads a zip over HTTPS with no checksum; the bytes are baked into the APK. One `sha256sum -c` line fixes it. *Resolved 2026-07-16: sha256 pinned in the script (cross-verified against Alpine aports' sha512 pin of the same artifact); the new in-app on-demand download (`VoiceModelDownloader`) verifies the same pin before install.* |
| S11 | M | **[judgment] Saathi mesh is plaintext broadcast with spoofable sender.** Mesh messages are unencrypted application-layer JSON visible to every LAN peer, and `MeshMessage.sender` is a free-text self-declared label (`saathi.rs:41-59,289-291`) not bound to the libp2p identity. Fine for a demo; incompatible with the README's privacy framing if ever wired up (currently it is not — see A1). |
| S12 | M | **[judgment] Untrusted DMs auto-compile to payment URIs.** Any inbound DM containing `/pay …` becomes a rendered UPI chip with a ready-made `upi://pay` URI (`vault.rs:34-50`, `desktop/ui/main.js:421-429`). Amount/VPA are shown, so the user does see what they'd approve, but money-adjacent parsing of attacker-controlled text deserves explicit confirm-flow design, and amounts are `f64` (see Q3). |

**Healthy:** AES-GCM usage is correct throughout (fresh random 96-bit nonce per seal, prepended; auth failure surfaces as typed errors) — `crypto.rs:123-149`, `storage/lib.rs:264-290`, `sakha.rs:39-65`. The ECDH x-only lift + SHA-256(x) + HKDF-labels construction (`crypto.rs:78-118`) composes standard primitives sensibly and is symmetric-verified by test. Storage's PIN verification fails closed (`StorageError::InvalidPin`), proven by tests including on-disk plaintext scans (`storage/lib.rs:346-370`, `repository.rs:377-408`).

## 4. Improvement Strategy

### Theme 1 — Close the promise/reality gap (drives N1, A1, A2, A3, A5, Q4)
**Target state:** every feature the README claims is either reachable from at least one frontend or explicitly labeled *experimental / not yet wired*. The state machine owns engine lifecycle: entering OffGridTravel disconnects relay engines (and, if product says so, starts Saathi); pairing actually calls `pair_with`; identity has one code path.
**Principle:** in a privacy product, credibility is the product. An honest smaller claim beats an aspirational bigger one — the false "relays paused" toast is worse than not having the feature.
**Trade-off:** wiring Saathi/media properly is weeks of work; re-scoping the README is an afternoon. Do the README first (M1), wire features by product priority later (M2+). **Do NOT** try to wire all three dormant engines at once.
**Done signals:** README table has an explicit status column; the Off-Grid toggle really disconnects (verifiable with `netstat`); Android can unlock + post + read timeline end-to-end; `sync_ledger` succeeds after a real pairing flow; engines with no non-test call sites are flagged experimental and out of the feature table.

### Theme 2 — Make the crypto match the marketing (S1, S2, S3, S8, N2)
**Target state:** DMs on NIP-44 (send + receive; NIP-04 kept read-only for backward compat), crash-safe re-keying (done — see D1/M1-2), a passphrase policy with strength feedback, and a `SECURITY.md` threat model that states plainly what is and isn't protected (plaintext keys in storage tables, no forward secrecy for Sakha, LAN-visible mesh).
**Principle:** composing audited primitives (which this codebase already does well) is necessary but not sufficient — protocol choice and key-management lifecycle are where privacy products actually fail.
**Trade-off:** skip a Sakha ratchet (Signal-style forward secrecy) for now — document it instead; it's XL work with low prototype payoff. **Do NOT** raise Argon2 params before profiling on the low-end Android target (unlock latency is UX-visible).
**Done signals:** new DMs are NIP-44-encrypted (gift-wrap decision recorded); a kill-the-process-mid-`change_pin` test passes; passphrase < 8 chars rejected at every frontend; `SECURITY.md` exists and matches the code.

### Theme 3 — CI and releases cover what can break (T1, T2, O1, O2, O3, D2, D3)
**Target state:** one PR gate that compiles/tests all three frontends: cargo lane (exists) + `gradle test` lane + `cargo check` of `desktop/src-tauri` + cargo-audit; wrapper committed; LICENSE decided; releases signed with a stable key and shipping the voice model (or explicitly labeled voice-less).
**Principle:** the safety net comes before the refactors it protects (this is milestone M0 for a reason); a release pipeline that produces non-upgradeable artifacts is worse than none.
**Trade-off:** don't build APKs or run emulator tests on PRs — JVM unit tests and compile checks give 90% of the signal at 10% of the cost. **Do NOT** add coverage-percentage gates yet; the test culture is already good, and gates on a moving prototype create ceremony.
**Done signals:** a deliberately broken Kotlin test fails CI; a deliberately broken Tauri command signature fails CI; CI fails on a crate with a known RUSTSEC advisory; two consecutive releases install as an upgrade on the same device.

### Theme 4 — Feed, persistence, and memory discipline (P1, P2, P3, Q2, Q4)
**Target state:** the Sabha subscription is scoped (follow set + limit, never author-unbounded), in-memory caches are ring buffers, incoming chitthis/DMs are persisted to the already-built repositories with backfill since last-seen, and blocking work is off the async threads.
**Principle:** unbounded anything (subscription, Vec, DOM) is a latent outage; the repository layer for persistence already exists and is the best-tested code in the repo — use it.
**Done signals:** desktop stays responsive against a live relay for 10 minutes; `state.chitthis` and Vault inbox have hard caps; killing and restarting the desktop app shows DMs received while it was closed (or the limitation is documented); no lock is held across a KDF or network await.

### What NOT to fix (explicit)
- **The three thin bridge layers** (CLI/JNI/Tauri command shims) — they duplicate marshalling, not logic; unifying them buys nothing. (Making the CLI depend on `comrade_ui` is worthwhile only as part of M2 wiring work, not as its own project.)
- ~~Sled → redb migration~~ — **done 2026-07-12** (see decision log); no longer deferred.
- **UPI feature hardening beyond f64 → paise** — product direction is unclear (see Open Questions); don't invest until it is.
- **Gendered `PairRole` naming, Hindi nomenclature** — product/brand decisions, not engineering defects; flagged in Open Questions only.
- **Triple nostr clients (P5), AES helper triplication (Q7), `comrade_state` history growth** — real but cheap debt; batch them into M3 or into adjacent M2 work, don't schedule standalone.

### Top-3 implementation sketches

**M1-1 · NIP-44 migration.**
Approach: nostr-sdk 0.44 exposes `nip44::encrypt/decrypt` and gift-wrap helpers. Replace `send_dm`'s `nip04::encrypt` (`vault.rs:118`) with NIP-44 payload encryption; ideally wrap in NIP-59 gift wrap (Kind 1059) so recipient metadata is also hidden — that changes the inbox filter from Kind 4 to Kind 1059. In the notification handler, branch on kind: 1059 → unwrap+NIP-44; 4 → legacy `nip04::decrypt` (read-only). Keys: gift-wrap uses ephemeral keys per message — nostr-sdk handles this. Steps: (1) add `send_dm_nip44`, migrate `send_dm` to it; (2) extend subscription filter to both kinds; (3) split decrypt paths; (4) tests: NIP-44 round-trip, official NIP-44 test vectors, legacy NIP-04 still readable; (5) update README row and error variants (fixes Q6 partially). Gotchas: gift-wrap timestamps are randomized (spec) — don't assert `created_at` ordering in tests; interop with other clients only works if they also do 1059 — decide plain-NIP-44-in-Kind-4 vs full gift-wrap in review (OQ4); `.since(now)` interacts with gift-wrap randomized timestamps (subscribe with a ~2-day skew window per spec).

**M1-2 · Atomic `change_pin`.**
Approach: staged re-encryption with a version marker. Steps: (1) write `rekey_state = {new_salt, new_verify_token, status: "in-progress"}` to META under a *new* key slot while keeping old salt/token valid; (2) copy every tree's values re-encrypted into `<tree>__rekey` siblings; (3) single meta write flips `active_salt` → new + `status: done`; (4) swap trees (rename or read-indirection) and drop old ones; (5) on `open`, detect stale `in-progress` → discard `__rekey` trees and open with the old key (rollback semantics). Convert M0-5's xfail test into: kill after each phase (inject a failpoint closure between steps), reopen, assert either fully-old or fully-new, never mixed. Gotchas: sled has no cross-tree transactions — the single-key meta flip is the linearization point, so the flip must be one `insert` of one composite value (not two); `Zeroizing` the old key after the flip; document that concurrent writers must be excluded during rekey (take `&mut self` — already the case).

**M1-5 · Scoped Sabha feed.**
Approach: make the filter an explicit input instead of a hardcoded firehose. Steps: (1) `subscribe_chitthi_feed(filter_spec, cb)` where `FilterSpec { authors: Vec<PublicKey>, since_secs, limit }`; default from stored contacts + own key (the `Contact` repository already exists, `repository.rs:142-159` — this also gives it its first production caller); fall back to `limit(100)` + own-key-only when contacts are empty — never author-unbounded; (2) in `runtime.rs:243` build the spec from the store at unlock; (3) cap `state.chitthis` in `main.js` (e.g. 500, drop tail on prepend) and cap the Vault inbox Vec symmetrically (P3); (4) soak-test against a live relay behind an `--ignored` integration test. Gotchas: an empty-follow cold start means an empty feed — product may want a curated bootstrap list instead (flag at review); the `since` replay on reconnect can double-deliver — the desktop's `seenChitthi` set already dedups, keep server-side `limit` modest so replay is cheap.

Session-style calling for the loved-one pillar. Design sketch, honest about
size (this is a **multi-PR epic**, comparable to everything built so far):

- **Media**: WebRTC — the only realistic cross-platform stack. Android:
  `org.webrtc` (Google's prebuilt libwebrtc AAR, ~10 MB — dwarfs our whole
  `.so`); desktop: the webview's built-in WebRTC (`getUserMedia` etc. work
  inside Tauri), so the desktop side is mostly JS.
- **Signaling**: SDP offers/answers + ICE candidates as ephemeral encrypted
  events over the existing Vault DM channel (the community direction is the
  NIP-100 draft; we'd carry the same payloads over NIP-04 now, NIP-44 after
  M1-1). No new infrastructure needed for signaling.
- **The hard truth — NAT traversal**: P2P connects directly for maybe
  60-70% of real-world pairs (STUN only, e.g. public Google/Cloudflare
  STUN). The rest (CGNAT — very common on Indian mobile carriers) need a
  **TURN relay**, which is real server infrastructure someone must run and
  pay for, and which sees (encrypted) media flow metadata. Session solves
  this with its own onion network; we don't have one.
- **Sequencing**: (1) voice-only, STUN-only, 1:1 — honest "call may not
  connect on some networks" UX; (2) TURN decision (OQ10); (3) video;
  (4) group calls never (SFU territory — out of scope).
- **OQ10:** who runs TURN? Options: none (accept ~30-40% connect failure),
  self-hosted coturn (cost + ops + a server that sees IP pairs), or a paid
  TURN service (fastest, least private). Owner call before starting calls.
  (**2026-07-15:** the self-hosted-coturn option is now turnkey —
  `deploy/coturn/`, time-limited REST credentials via
  `comrade_core::call::mint_turn_rest_credentials` — which removes the
  *engineering* lift. Who operates it, and its ongoing cost, is still the
  owner's call.)

### 8.2 Watch party — listen/watch together (owner request, 2026-07-12)

Shared media consumption with the loved one / small groups. The sync problem
is small; the content problem is the real constraint:

- **What we can build cleanly**: a *sync-play control channel* — play,
  pause, seek, position-heartbeat messages over the existing E2E DM channel
  (or the Sakha CRDT for couple state). Each participant plays **their own
  copy or their own stream** of the content; Comrade only synchronises the
  clocks (drift correction on heartbeat, target < 300 ms — DM-over-relay
  latency is fine for play/pause, marginal for tight seek-sync; a later
  WebRTC data channel from 8.1 would make it tight).
- **What we must not build**: re-streaming or proxying licensed
  audio/video between users. That's both a copyright problem and a
  bandwidth problem. DRM'd platform content (Netflix/Spotify) cannot be
  frame-synced inside our app at all — the platforms prohibit and
  technically block it. The honest v1 targets: local files both sides
  already have, and embeddable sources (e.g. YouTube via the official
  embed player API, which exposes play/pause/seek).
- **v1 scope**: "Listen together" for a shared YouTube link or local audio

> Three of this section's own assumptions did not survive the code, and the
> build follows the corrections rather than the sketch:
>
> - **"< 300 ms" is not reachable over a relay**, and the reason is sharper than
>   latency. A DM's `created_at` is whole seconds on the sender's wall clock and
>   nothing in the repo estimated clock offset, so each heartbeat now carries the
>   timestamps of the one before it (an NTP probe at zero extra messages).
>   Measuring the offset also measures its error, giving the rule the module is
>   built around — *never correct by less than your own measurement error*. Half
>   a public relay's round trip routinely exceeds 300 ms, so that target sits
>   under the noise floor. Held instead: **±0.3–0.8 s** on a local file.
> - **A fast position heartbeat is paid for twice.** Every beat is a persistent
>   gift wrap and the inbox rewinds two days on every launch, so the cadence
>   decides how much each later start re-downloads. 10 s, none while paused,
>   because two local players drift only by crystal error (tens of ppm).
> - **The loved-one-space precondition does not apply**, because a session is
>   scoped to any accepted conversation and gated exactly like call signaling.
>   (The pairing claim it rested on was also stale — see below.)
>
